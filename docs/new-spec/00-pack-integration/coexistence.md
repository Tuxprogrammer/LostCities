# Coexistence: building cities around other world generation

Cities share the GTNH 2.9.0 overworld with other mods' structures. Under decision log D24, a city gets out of their way: it builds around them so they become features of the city, it sews nearby villages in with paths, and it never leaves a sharp cutoff. This sheet answers, for every surface structure generator in the pack: can its site, footprint and ground height be predicted without generating chunks, and what does the city do about it. It builds on the inventory in [other-worldgen.md](other-worldgen.md), which carries its corrections, and is the research behind record [0006](../../design/0006-build-around-other-worldgen.md).

Status: *Accepted* ([0006](../../design/0006-build-around-other-worldgen.md); [decision log](../../design/decision-log.md) D26; the margin, soft edges and lanes revised by the alpha review round, D149 to D153). Facts about other mods are *verified* with a permalink at the pack's tag, *decompiled* (closed source; the contract only, no code), *vanilla* (read from the 1.7.10 server with Forge 10.13.4.1614 applied; no public pinned source), or *unverified*.

## The model this sheet assumes

[0006](../../design/0006-build-around-other-worldgen.md) decides it; in short:

- A **landmark** is another generator's structure the layout knows about through a pure **structure oracle**. It has a footprint (rectangles in block X and Z), a ground estimate, a certainty (exact, union of possibilities, or predicted candidate) and, for villages, **gates** (where its paths can meet ours).
- Its **core** is every chunk the footprint, grown by a margin of 2 blocks, touches. The city writes nothing in a core chunk but a village lane's surface path: no building, street, foundation, cellar, corridor, terrain blend or decoration clearing. RWG's terrain and decoration stay as RWG made them.
- Its **edge ring** is the city's **margin**: the chunks next to the core (eight neighbors). They are never city: the city builds inside them. They are natural ground, sloped by the blend from the city's edge to the core's ground, with a **soft-edge strip** that meets the core's own edge columns, and nothing city-made but a village lane down the slope (alpha review AR-10).
- Villages also get **transition rings** of lower density and **lanes** from city streets to the village's paths.

### Predictability

- **Site:** pure. The grid needs the world seed and the VillageNames config; the biome test needs only the biome provider, which on RWG2 is a pure function of the seed ([rwg.md](rwg.md)).
- **Footprint:** pure, once the handler order is pinned (D86). VillageNames' `getStructureStart`, called with its generator's `Random` in the state `MapGenBase` leaves for the chunk, gives every piece's box in X and Z, the town center's position and type, and every path. `getStructureStart` is protected; the oracle reaches it from a private subclass of `MapGenVillageVN`, so no mixin is needed (D84).
- **Measured** with the `oracle.villagenames` scenario on fresh real-pack worlds, seed 4242: on RWG2 Continents, 71 of 169 grid cells around chunk (−4096, 4096) start a village (VillageNames' list holds BoP and vanilla names such as Frost Forest, Snow Forest and Meadow, which RWG2 reports; its own names such as `Plains (RWG)` are not in the list), and all 6 villages generated matched VillageNames' saved plans piece for piece, with no village it recorded unpredicted and no rejected start holding one. On classic RWG, 95 of the same 169 cells start a village (Rainforest, Snow Forest, Sacred Springs, Bamboo Forest), all 6 generated matched piece for piece, and no monument was predicted or recorded. Two fresh worlds of one seed print the same oracle hash ([testing](../../testing/README.md#villagenames-oracle-runs-on-the-real-pack); decision log D90).
- **Ground height:** not pure. Each piece reads its own ground from the world at population. Because the city never writes in a village core, that ground is RWG's, and the height oracle's median over the box estimates it. Caves and RWG's decoration are the error.
- **What VillageNames exposes:** no API for queries. The start (`StructureStart.getComponents()`, `getBoundingBox()`), each piece's box and facing, the start's village and material type, and `PathVN` pieces are reachable on the objects. The saved `data/Village.dat` holds every start computed so far, but it fills in generation order, so the layout must not read it.
- **What we predict ourselves:** the grid, its seed, the village-free radius and the biome test, reimplemented in `compat.villagenames.VillageGrid` (int arithmetic kept, overflow included), and the per-chunk generator seed (`MapGenSeeds`). It never calls `canSpawnStructureAtCoords` on a live generator, because that reseeds `world.rand`. The plan itself is VillageNames' own: the oracle seeds its private generator and calls `getStructureStart` (D84).
- **Side effects of a private construction:** the plan calls `BiomeManager.addVillageBiome` (which generation does anyway) and every registered village handler's piece-weight hook with the passed `Random`; nothing reads or writes the world, saved data or `world.rand`. The `oracle.villagenames` scenario checks that a scan loads no chunk and leaves `world.rand` as it was, and that the plans equal what VillageNames then saves ([testing](../../testing/README.md#scenario-list)).

### Sewing a village into a city

1. **Core.** Every chunk any piece box (grown by 2 blocks) touches. Nothing of the city is written there. The village builds itself on RWG's ground as it would in the wild.
2. **Margin (edge ring).** The chunks next to the core: never city. The blend slopes them from the city's ground to the core's, a soft-edge strip meets the core's edge columns, and RWG's trees stay. A street that runs toward the village ends in a plaza in the city chunk before the margin.
3. **Transition rings.** The next two rings of city chunks, as profile options: building chance halved, at most 2 floors in the second ring and 4 in the third. Open lots become parks as upstream's hierarchical open lots do. The village reads as the city's old town. A village inside a city's radius is surrounded: the city continues on every side of it rather than stopping short.
4. **Gates.** A gate is the end of a village path that faces the city: a `PathVN` end that no other piece continues, or a path box within one chunk of the core's edge. Each side of the core gets at most one gate, the one nearest the city's center; ties go north, then west.
5. **Lanes.** For each gate, the gate chunk is the margin chunk in front of it. The lane crosses it straight and, in a city, goes on from the city chunk beyond it to the nearest effective city road: straight, or an L with the bend toward the city's center, through transition chunks, which are forced to the tertiary road class; a secondary or tertiary road is preferred to a primary one up to 2 chunks farther, so a village is sewn in with small roads (AR-7). In the gate chunk a 3-block-wide surface path (with the path block chosen per column by VillageNames' own path rule, so lane and village path match: D31, [03-terrain](../03-terrain/index.md)) runs from the city street, through an opening in its border, down or up the margin to the core's edge, in line with the village path. Its columns are graded in a straight ramp from the street's ground to the core's edge first, the column on each side meeting it halfway, and wherever the path rises a block it gets a stair, or a half slab on a single step (AR-11).
6. **Inside the core**, the lane continues as a surface-only path (the path block replaces the natural top block; nothing is written above or below it) to meet the village path's end, never within 2 blocks of a piece box.
7. **Villages outside the city** but within `villageLinkDistance` (4 chunks) of its edge get the same gates and a lane across the non-city land between them, written as a surface path in the terrain phase. Farther villages are left alone.
8. **Severed roads.** Primary roads that cross a village core or its margin detour through the ring of city chunks just outside the margin on the shorter side (ties north, then west); secondary and tertiary roads, and a primary road with no detour, end in a plaza facing the margin. No major road runs through the margin.
9. **City level** near a village comes from the same oracle heights as the village's own ground, so the margin's slope usually closes a step of one city level (6 blocks) or less over its 16 columns.

**Predictability.** The candidate list is pure. Which candidate wins depends on blocks: caves or lakes under the 9 × 9 layers, and tall grass (which shifts G by one) are things only the world knows. Running the 50 candidates against the biome provider and the height oracle predicts the winner where the terrain is plain, and is wrong where a cave, a lake, RWG's boulders or a shore one block lower than the oracle's height decides. Measured with `oracle.roguelike` on the real pack (seed 4242, the 25 cells around chunk (0, 0), every search's chunks generated first): the tower stood at the predicted site, or neither was predicted nor built, in 15 of 25 cells on classic RWG (60 %) and 17 of 25 on RWG2 Continents (68 %); the misses were towers that moved to a later candidate in the same cell (8 and 6) and predicted sites with no tower (2 and 2), never a tower where none was predicted. Every tower's lot was clear of city chunks and edge rings; the steering refused 145 and 120 points in 13 and 11 cells. In the pack Roguelike's search must run where reading a missing chunk generates it, as Hodgepodge's chunk-generation scheduler lets population do: run inside the server tick, outside that window, it reads missing chunks as empty air and finds no site.

**Treatment: build around a predicted lot, and steer.** Built in M08 (`compat.roguelike`).
- The structure oracle (`RoguelikeOracle`) computes each cell's dungeon chunk with `world.setRandomSeed`'s formula on a `Random` of its own, of `world.rand`'s class (never `world.rand` itself), and the 50 candidates from FML's generator seed (`(xSeed × cx + zSeed × cz) ^ worldSeed`, `xSeed` and `zSeed` the first two `nextLong() >> 3` of `new Random(worldSeed)`; nothing in the pack replaces that `Random`). It tests them in order with `validLocation`'s rules estimated on the biome provider and the height oracle (`SiteRules`): a column is solid up to the oracle height, its valid ground one lower under a grass top, water stands over every column below sea level. The first that passes is a landmark `roguelike@<x>,<z>` with certainty *candidate*: a lot of 21 × 21 blocks around the point (the widest tower plus margin), its ground the oracle height. Roguelike's own config (`RogueConfig`) supplies the frequency, the Y limits and the dimension lists; they are pinned in the profile snapshot.
- A late mixin on `Dungeon.validLocation` (`mixins.late.MixinRoguelikeDungeon`, head injection, applied only with Roguelike loaded) refuses a point whose 21 × 21 lot touches a city chunk, an edge-ring chunk or a scattered structure's footprint (D31; none exist before M07), unless the point is a predicted site and no city chunk lies under its lot. Under `BUILD_AROUND` the predicted lot is a core, so it is never refused; under `IGNORE` the city covers it and the tower is refused like any other point. The dungeon therefore lands in its lot, outside the city, or not at all. Every input is the pure layout, so the outcome follows the layout, not the order chunks are generated in. It also steers `/roguelike dungeon` near a city, since that command runs the same search.
- If the real winner fails in the lot (a cave), the lot stays natural ground and reads as a park.
- The levels below Y 50 coexist (T3), as mineshafts do. The deepest cellars (city level 0 with three cellars reaches Y 53) can meet the top level's rooms.
- The real-pack check is `oracle.roguelike` ([docs/testing](../../testing/README.md)); its measured hit rate is under [Predictability](#roguelike-dungeons) above.

## RWG2 landmarks

Volcano vicinity, lava-cave openings (70-block surface radius) and mountain-chain river junctions (strength ≥ 0.70 near a mountain chain) are pure predicates on the chunk manager; RWG2 itself uses them, with an 8-block margin, to keep Witchery off its landmarks ([WitcheryWorldgenCompat.java#L64-L95](https://github.com/GTNewHorizons/Realistic-World-Gen-2/blob/2.0.6/src/main/java/rwg2/support/WitcheryWorldgenCompat.java#L64-L95); [ChunkManagerRealistic.java#L413-L428](https://github.com/GTNewHorizons/Realistic-World-Gen-2/blob/2.0.6/src/main/java/rwg2/world/ChunkManagerRealistic.java#L413-L428), [#L912](https://github.com/GTNewHorizons/Realistic-World-Gen-2/blob/2.0.6/src/main/java/rwg2/world/ChunkManagerRealistic.java#L912)). **Treatment: terrain landmark.** The columns the predicates mark, grown by RWG2's own 8-block margin, are a landmark footprint, and every chunk that footprint (grown by `landmarkMargin`) touches is core. The world's own `ChunkManagerRealistic`, whose predicates are pure (its maps only remember answers), answers them; the height oracle reads the same manager ([0005](../../design/0005-rwg-overlay.md) §4).

The biome test runs while the start chunk is populated, through `World.getBiomeGenForCoords`, so it reads that chunk's stored biome array, which RWG dithers near biome borders; the oracle reads the biome provider, which agrees everywhere else (decision log D91). The standard world style multiplies city chance by 0.1 in ocean biomes ([standard.json#L83-L91](https://github.com/McJtyMods/LostCities/blob/f18a094d/src/main/resources/data/lostcities/lostcities/worldstyles/standard.json#L83-L91)), so cities seldom reach deep ocean, but the oil rig, a scattered structure for deep ocean ([07-scattered](../07-scattered/index.md#scattered-structures)), can share a site. **Treatment: landmark core** (D32), built in `compat.villagenames.MonumentOracle`: an `EXACT` core of the 58 × 58 building, with its edge ring. On the real pack (RWG2 Continents, seed 4242) 89 of 1,681 grid cells around chunk (−4096, 4096) were predicted; of the 8 generated, 7 stood where predicted and 1 did not, because its chunk's stored biomes fail the test (explained, D91); VillageNames recorded no monument the oracle missed. A predicted monument that does not appear leaves natural sea, as any empty lot does (0006 §5).
- **Unchanged from [other-worldgen.md](other-worldgen.md):** T2 denial of `DUNGEON`, `LAKE`, `LAVA`, `CUSTOM` (NHCore oil) and `RAILCRAFT_QUARRY` in city chunks, and of the first four where a window touches a scattered footprint (D31); T3 for strongholds, mineshafts, geodes, small decoration and slime islands. Core chunks are not city chunks, so T2 does not reach them: a lake or dungeon there is part of the natural landscape, as next to any village in the wild.

## What upstream's own avoidance gives, and what it lacks

Upstream 1.20's `StructureAvoidance` ([structure-avoidance.md](../../original-spec/07-scattered/structure-avoidance.md); [StructureAvoidance.java](https://github.com/McJtyMods/LostCities/blob/f18a094d/src/main/java/mcjty/lostcities/worldgen/lost/StructureAvoidance.java)):

| Upstream gives | Here |
|---|---|
| Chunks holding a village (or a listed structure) are not city; with the defaults, a one-chunk ring around them is not city either | Kept: the core, and the ring as the city's margin, natural ground sloped from the city to the core and crossed by lanes |
| `avoidFlattening`: terrain in the structure's own chunk is not blended | Kept: core chunks are never written, blended or cleared |
| The non-city ring is blended by the normal edge blend toward the city | Kept and anchored: the blend is fixed to the core's natural ground at the core's corners, and a soft-edge strip in the margin meets the core's own edge columns |
| Multibuildings are decided per footprint | Kept: a multibuilding may not cover a core or edge-ring chunk |
| It learns structures from 1.20's per-chunk structure references, which exist before the city is written | 1.7.10 has none, and other mods' structures are written after the city. The structure oracle predicts them instead |
| Its result depends on which neighbors have references yet (`UNKNOWN`, SCAT-02, SCAT-03), and its window is shifted (SCAT-01) | The oracle is pure and always known; the window is defined fresh. None of the four bugs can occur |
| Streets: none. Roads into an avoided chunk are clipped like any road leaving the city | Plazas, detours for primary roads, and lanes to villages |

## Order and determinism at the seams

| Seam | What happens | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Our terrain phase runs before every other structure's blocks exist | Other structures' pieces are written in RWG's `populate` or in `IWorldGenerator`s, after our terrain phase and before or after our population phase | The layout never reads blocks other mods placed. Each oracle reads only the world seed, coordinates, the biome provider, the height oracle and other mods' config |
| A structure lands where the oracle said | It builds on RWG's untouched ground in its core | None needed |
| A structure is smaller than its lot or does not appear (Witchery picks a small type; RWG2's mixin rejects; LootGames finds a tile entity; a Roguelike candidate fails) | The core keeps natural ground and RWG's decoration | Reads as a green; the margin's natural ground and soft edges make it look intended |
| A structure lands somewhere the oracle did not predict | Roguelike only, for a cave or lake under a candidate | The steer mixin rejects any point inside a city chunk or edge ring other than its own lot |
| A structure writes into a city chunk anyway | Thaumcraft mounds in parks; anything a future pack version adds | Accepted at alpha; if screenshots show it, a guard (T4) for that generator, never a restore pass over the other mod's blocks |
| Our population phase after another structure's | Our population phase writes only inside our own footprint, never in a core | The write guard of [0005](../../design/0005-rwg-overlay.md) §3a keeps RWG's decoration out of our footprint; it does not act in cores, so RWG's decoration in a core stays |
| Other mods' config changes on an existing world | Villages, Witchery and Roguelike sites move; already-generated chunks keep the old ones | The values each oracle reads are part of the pinned profile snapshot ([0005](../../design/0005-rwg-overlay.md) §7); a mismatch is logged at load |
| Oracles that would reseed `world.rand` | `canSpawnStructureAtCoords` on a live village generator, `world.setRandomSeed` | Never called: the oracle computes vanilla's seed formula itself, so Witchery's `world.rand` stream is not disturbed |

## Soft edges

Upstream softens the city's outer edge with its terrain blend ([terrain-blend.md](../../original-spec/03-terrain/terrain-blend.md)): non-city chunks within two chunks of a city are reshaped between a lower and an upper height mesh pinned to the city ground at shared corners. Inside the city, a street next to a lower neighbor gets a border wall. These rules carry that softness to every edge a landmark creates:

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| E1 | **Cores are fixed ground.** A core chunk is never reshaped. In the blend, a corner shared by a core chunk and a non-city chunk is a fixed corner whose lower and upper heights are the core's own oracle height (offsets −1 to +1), the same way a city corner is fixed at the city ground. The blend between a city and a nearby landmark is spread over the chunks between them. |
| E2 | **The city builds inside a margin; the strip stitches it to the core.** The city's chunks stop a chunk short of a core. In the margin chunk between, after the blend (E1) has sloped it from the city's ground toward the core's oracle height, a soft-edge strip of `softEdgeWidth` columns (default 6) ramps the blended ground to each core-side edge column's own natural height, read before the blend, keeping the column's own surface block. Where water stands on the edge column (a pond or river in the core), its natural height is the water's surface: the strip meets it at the waterline (D129). Nothing the city builds lies in the margin, so no slope ever cuts a road, a highway or a park (AR-10, AR-12, AR-13). |
| E3 | **Steep steps stay walkable or honest.** The city's street chunks keep upstream's border toward the margin where it is lower, opened where a lane arrives. A plan that puts a city chunk right beside a core (only the synthetic test city) gets the strip in the city chunk instead, ending in a retaining border of the city style's border block where the step is more than twice the strip, at most as tall as the remaining step. |
| E4 | **No straight lines.** The strip's width is varied by upstream's transition noise (±2 columns), as the blend meshes are. |
| E5 | **Green on both sides.** The margin is not city and lies outside the city footprint ([0005](../../design/0005-rwg-overlay.md) §3a), so RWG's trees and boulders stay in it, continuing the core's own decoration. |
| E6 | **Density falls off.** Next to a core, no buildings; for villages, two further rings of lower density (building chance × 0.5; at most 2, then 4 floors). Crater basins cap heights instead. |
| E7 | **Nothing underground.** The margin is not city, so it has no cellars, corridors, subways or rail dungeons, and nothing city-made shows in a core's cave or under the strip. |
| E8 | **Paths meet paths.** Lanes are 3 blocks wide, the width of a village path. Across the margin a lane is graded in a straight ramp from the city street's ground to the core's edge and gets a stair, or a half slab on a single step, wherever it rises a block, so people would walk it (AR-7, AR-11). |

## Decisions

Settled by the owner in decision log D26 and recorded in [0006](../../design/0006-build-around-other-worldgen.md):

1. **Lanes inside village chunks:** a surface-only lane may cross a village's core chunks to meet the village path, never within 2 blocks of a piece box.
2. **AE2 meteorites:** a meteorite that lands in a city gets a crater basin the city steps down toward.
3. **Roguelike Dungeons:** the steering mixin is built in M08, not deferred to the alpha screenshots.
4. **Villages inside a city's radius:** the city surrounds them, and each becomes the city's old town.
5. **Scattered structures** (D31): the T2 denial of dungeons, lakes, lava and oil, and the Roguelike steering mixin, extend to scattered footprints. The village lane's path block follows VillageNames' per-column rule (D31).
