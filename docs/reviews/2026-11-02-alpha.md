- **M12** means it waits for City edges and character, before beta.

## The owner's notes

> **Alpha review: real cities on RWG2 Continents, seed 4242**
>
> **Above:** Overall details and structure look good. Notice a missing staircase step in the bottom right, should be stone slab there to complete the staircase
>
> **Street:** RWG terrain spawned a lot of stray grass blocks on the left hand side of the image muddying up the street, please clean the streets. Trees and other foliage look great.
>
> **Interior:** Looks fine for alpha, a little dull but that will be fixed in the palette adjustments during the push to beta.
>
> **Edge:** This needs some work, the buildings need to taper in height from the city center in an organic way. Having a 8 floor high rise abruptly end right next to a rising slope from a mountain is too harsh of a transition. on your road to beta, polish the transitions so that terrain fades from RWG into dense highrise city that LC generates. I suggest leaving a few "Empty" plots or Construction plots near the city edges to show planned growth that never happened in the city. on the empty plots prevent trees or foliage from growing in them and set them to a brown or red clay like block as perfect grass wouldn't be growing there, dig some of them down one or two or maybe 4 blocks to show digging out for a basement. Have a variant with concrete pillars showing the early stages of highrise building
> I am sort of worried about the giant wall that is the background, it looks like the generator took its "ground level" from the mountain in the background right but this is sort of weird looking and unrealistic for a city to have a ground level for buildings that is 10 or 12 stories high. we should be putting a cap on how high terrain can rise so that LC doesn't go wild and generate 40 or 60 block high parks.
>
> **Village sewing:** I like the heights and I like the sloping on the right hand side. The weak part here is the roads. The transitions are too harsh it goes from 3 or 4 block wide dirt path directly to 20 block wide major thoroughfare. We should sew cities with small roads so that the transition feels more natural. Also the sews should slope down, in the image the slope has no road blocks in it, it just hard cuts the roads at the top of the slop and connects to the village road at the bottom of the slope.
>
> As a general comment, I'm seeing lots of craters and city damage, lets turn that down here a lot. LC by default has its damage factor set too high for what look I'm going for, right now I think we have too many craters. Also none of the damage even looks partially repaired. I think you would gain a lot of realism to have damaged parts partially filled back in, perhaps on buildings with a similar but not identical material to show a sort of "this broke, but was filled back" new look that repair materials would show.
>
> **Reserved lot:** mostly the right look here. I can see where we didn't end the city soon enough so the park in the center right of the image has stone steps that end abruptly even though they cover three edges of the rise. it looks to me like the slopes are cutting into the city's generated terrain to buffer the city, I would rather have the city build in a margin, and then the slope stiches that margin into the village. that way we don't have abrupt endings of where a park is, where the sloping starts, and then where the village is. Look at how the major road is cut nearly in half because 6 blocks of slope is cutting into our highway, we shouldn't have put a highway that close to the village and a buffer margin would have helped us there. This shows the same abrupt road ending in slop from the other angle so you can see it on the far right side of the screen our highway just comes to and end. On this view it looks like a cliff but the slope is there hidden from our view. We should incorporate stairs into our slopes to keep the transition more gradual and realistic to what people would actually walk. Nobody is walking up and down 1 meter slopes, the stairs 0.5 meter is more believable.
>
> **Softedge:** Same complaints as before, highway is being eaten into by the slope this should never happen. very odd cliff like looking drop off on the left hand side of the screen.
>
> **Monument:** I agree we kept clear but you just showed me an ocean here, this screenshot only confirms we don't build random cities on top of ocean water. Looks fine but sort of pointless as a reviewer.
>
> **Alpha review: real cities on classic RWG, seed 4242**
>
> Lots of red in the buildings here mixed with sandstone is a little jarring, almost like we couldn't make our mind up if this was a desert city or a regular city. I think this might be why red sand or red clay was left out of desert cities. I prefer a more muted desert palette and this brings up the question of how we handle biome transitions, our build colors should feather, by this I mean the building's facade should be 1 color but the transitions of side-by-side buildings shouldn't be so abrupt that you put sandstone, a low saturation bright color next to a highly saturated dark red color building. I don't want this to become a rule of "only light buildings everywhere" but I also don't want this building style to happen again.
>
> **Above:** Again the same problem here from the RWG2 screenshots, you are showing that RWG generated sand in the city street muddying it up, please clean the city streets where players will be walking. The park in the bottom left is raised up too high, and the grass is a little bare only a couple flowers/sapplings there. It could use some bonemeal to fill it in some more.
>
> **Street:** A random beehive spawned on what we are calling a wall, this might have been totally random so no real action just noting it. Sand on the street needs cleaning up.
>
> **Interior:** Looks fine here, in a sandstone palette the bleached color is what we are going for, this is appropriate here.
>
> **Edge:** Same compiants as RWG2, this is way too abrupt as a city ending, our buildings are way too tall and that park is almost in the clouds its too tall, probably broken by the rwg mountain that is there in the background. If we could find a way to get a happy medium between totally cutting the RWG mountain down and raising our building level to the sky that would be much appreciated.
>
> **Village:** The sewing here is much better hidden because there is a bigger buffer between street and village, and we used smaller streets here. Still a little too big and the street seems to end when it probably could have kept going in the center of the image. The paths from the city look a lot more realistic here where they go up and down the slope and are randomized so that they don't look artificial.
>
> **Reserved:** sure, same complaint surfacing here, there was a park that got cut in half so several park features are missing. we should have just left those as blank grass rather than put stone sections just to cut them in half and leave one side of stone slabs off.

> **Softedge:** Most of this is totally correct, I love the smaller slope here so that its less jarring to go from city to village. what I'm seeing wrong is the massive stone brick column in the background top center of the photo, that is way too high and completely out of place.
