# 05 · Assets

## Overview

The JSON asset system of Lost Cities 1.20-7.5.7 (upstream `1.20` at `f18a094d`): the thirteen asset types (parts, buildings, multibuildings, palettes, variants, styles, city styles, conditions, world styles, scattered structures, stuff, predefined cities, predefined spheres), every JSON field with its default and validation, how assets are loaded from data packs, overridden, compiled and looked up, how a part's characters become blocks (block states, random lists, damaged blocks, loot, spawners, torches, block-entity NBT), how parts are rotated and mirrored, and how conditions pick loot tables, mobs and building parts. It also catalogs all 322 bundled asset files and inventories every block they and the generator place, with its 1.7.10 vanilla counterpart.

Behavior that consumes these assets (where buildings go, how many floors, street layout, scattered placement, world-style city selection) belongs to other areas; this area documents their formats and the shared machinery.

## Key facts

- **Assets are data-pack registries.** Files live at `data/<ns>/lostcities/<type>/<name>.json`; the id is `<ns>:<name>`; unqualified names in assets mean `lostcities:<name>`. A higher-priority pack replaces a file whole. Registries are read once per world; `/reload` does not change them.
- **Compilation is lazy and server-bound.** An asset is compiled the first time someone asks for it, against the running server's registries. Palettes resolve block strings at that moment, so a bad block name fails when the palette is first used, not at load.
- **A space in a part means "leave the block alone".** Air is never written from a part. `minecraft:structure_void` ("hard air", bundled `~` and `b`) is what clears space, or fills it with water below the water level.
- **Random lists have 128 slots**, filled by weight in list order; the last entry usually carries weight 1000 to fill the rest. Fewer than 128 slots is an error; extra weight is dropped.
- **Three palette layers per part:** the style's palette (one palette per group, later groups override), then the building's local palette, then the part's local palette.
- **Characters are global conventions.** All bundled parts draw from one shared character set ([catalog-palettes.md](catalog-palettes.md#character-conventions)); a new style must define all of them.
- **City styles inherit by appending.** A child city style copies unset scalar settings from its parent and appends the parent's selector lists to its own.
- **Only stairs, doors and rails rotate.** Other directional blocks keep their palette facing in rotated parts; torches are re-oriented after generation by looking at their neighbors.
- **Loot and mobs are indirect.** A palette's `loot` or `mob` names a condition asset, whose weighted values are loot-table ids or entity ids, guarded by floor, part, building, biome and sphere tests.
- **Heights.** Floors are 6 blocks. Assets carry no absolute heights except stuff `minheight`/`maxheight` (block Y) and condition `range` (floors). Tall parts: `radiotower` 24 slices, oil-rig parts 21 to 22.
- **No player-visible text.** The area has no lang keys and no textures; its error and log messages are hard-coded English and appear only in logs and crash reports.

## Related areas

- `01-core`: mod lifecycle that registers the registries and resets the caches; the editor and the `testfill`, part-export and create-building commands use compiled palettes.
- `02-profiles-dimensions`: profiles name the world style (`worldStyle`, default `standard`) and the alternative city style; world-style behavior (city-style choice per biome, city chance multipliers).
- `03-terrain`: the chunk driver that receives part blocks and recomputes connections; ruins, damage and explosions use the palettes' damaged blocks; city-sphere centers use the world style's `cityspheres`; vines use `settings`.
- `04-city-layout`: building and multibuilding placement, floor and cellar counts (`EffectiveCitySettings`), the building random that picks palettes and parts, streets, parks, fountains, fronts, stairs, corridors, predefined cities.
- `06-highways-railways`: highway, railway, station and monorail parts from the world style's part selector; bridges and supports.
- `07-scattered`: scattered structures and stuff (formats here, behavior there).
- `08-loot-mobs-blocks`: the loot tables named by `chestloot`, the mobs named by `easymobs`/`hardmobs`, and the six block tags (`rotatable`, `lights`, `needspoi`, `foliage`, `easybreakable`, `notbreakable`).
- `09-client-ui`: the world-creation preview must not compile palettes on the client ([ASSET-14](#suspected-bugs)).

## Known limits

- Nothing was run. Block placement, appearance and the descriptions of what parts depict are *inferred* from code, slices and palettes.
- Two library behaviors are inferred, not read: that Forge's block registry answers unknown ids with air (basis of [ASSET-13](#suspected-bugs)), and what vanilla `BlockStateData.upgradeBlock` maps.
- The 1.7.10 metadata values in [block-inventory.md](block-inventory.md) come from the vanilla 1.12 → 1.13 flattening layout, not from reading 1.7.10 code or the GTNH pack; the new spec should confirm each against the pack.
- McJty's online asset documentation was not cross-checked against the code; the code at the pin is the source.
- The "On 1.12" notes come from reading `upstream/1.12` asset classes, not from running that version.
- Whether exceptions thrown inside codec constructors (empty `char`, unknown sphere `centertype`) show the data-pack error screen or crash depends on Minecraft's data-pack loading, which was not traced.
